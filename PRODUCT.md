# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Hiring managers and technical recruiters evaluating Fabio Campos for software
engineering roles and internships. They arrive from a resume, a LinkedIn message,
or an application link, usually on a laptop, usually with a stack of other
candidates open in adjacent tabs. They skim first and read second. The job they
are trying to get done is fast and unglamorous: decide within roughly thirty
seconds whether this person is worth a real conversation, and find enough
concrete evidence to justify that decision to someone else.

## Product Purpose

A single-page personal portfolio site for Fabio Campos, B.S. Electrical and
Computer Engineering at Carnegie Mellon. It presents his experience, projects,
and skills as evidence of engineering rigor. Success is a recruiter reaching out
by email.

## Positioning

An engineer who does not just build things, but builds them properly, and the
site itself is the first exhibit.

## Brand Personality

Rigorous, precise, and quietly confident. The voice is a systems engineer's:
specific, unhurried, allergic to hype. The site should feel engineered rather
than decorated, and the intended feeling a recruiter leaves with is that this
person is unusually rigorous.

## Anti-references

Not a generic developer portfolio template.

## Design Principles

- **Rigor is demonstrated, not claimed.** The interface's own craft is the argument. Nothing on the page should say "detail-oriented"; the page should be the evidence.
- **Fidelity over pastiche.** The PlayStation 5 system UI is the reference. Translate its actual mechanics — focus, ambient background, the activity feed — not a superficial impression of "gamer dark mode."
- **Focus is the interaction model.** A console UI has exactly one focused element, and everything else responds to it. That single idea should drive navigation, projects, and keyboard support alike.
- **Evidence over inventory.** Five to ten pieces of work presented with depth beat a comprehensive list presented flatly.
- **The theme must never cost legibility.** Blur, glow, and darkness are in service of readability, never at its expense.
